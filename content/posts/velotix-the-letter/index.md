## The Delivery

On Friday 10 July 2026 at 16:37 Israel time, a WhatsApp message arrived from an Israeli mobile number ending 0189. The sender identified himself as a courier acting for a firm of advocates:

> גיא שלום רב
>
> אני שליח ממשרד עורכי דין הרשקוביץ ושות'. הגעתי כדי למסור לך מכתב התראה לפני הגשת תביעה, ביום א הקרוב, אך הבנתי שאתה לא בבית. בהתאם להנחיות הלקוח ולאחר דחיפות העניין התבקשתי להעביר לך עותק של המכתב. בוואטס אפ. מצ"ב עותק המכתב שימסר לך גם במסירה אישית בהמשך.
>
> שבת שלום.

> Guy, hello.
>
> I am a courier from the law offices of Hershkovich & Co. I came to serve you with a warning letter prior to the filing of a claim, this coming Sunday, but I understood that you are not at home. In accordance with the client's instructions, and given the urgency of the matter, I was asked to send you a copy of the letter. By WhatsApp. Attached is a copy of the letter, which will also be served on you personally in due course.
>
> Shabbat shalom.

The message was marked as forwarded. It was preceded by four messages that the WhatsApp bridge could not decrypt and one of a type it could not render. It was followed by two photographs, each 1080×1920, of a creased A4 page held flat against a floor. The pages are the two sides of a letter dated 9 July 2026.

The letterhead reads `HG — HERSHKOVICH&CO LAW FIRM`.

TK — verification of the firm and of the signatory against the Israel Bar Association register is outstanding at the time of writing. This post should not go live until that is done, and this paragraph should be replaced by what the register says. A letter photographed on a floor and forwarded over WhatsApp by a courier who could not find the addressee at home is not personal service, and until the register is checked, the document's provenance rests on the assertion of an unverified sender.

The letter is written on behalf of two clients jointly:

Dr Hod, who is named in the letter as a client in his own right and not solely as an officer of the company, is the subject of [Part 2](/posts/velotix-the-ceo/).

The subject line:

> הנדון: התראה טרם נקיטת הליכים משפטיים — פרסומי לשון הרע, שקר מפגיע, תיאור כוזב, פגיעה במוניטין, הפרת התחייבויות חוזיות וגרימת נזקים כבדים

> Re: Warning prior to the taking of legal proceedings — publications of defamation, injurious falsehood, false description, harm to reputation, breach of contractual undertakings, and the causing of severe damages.

Five causes of action, before a single sentence of the series has been quoted.

Clause 1 identifies the publications by date: 20.03.2026, 21.03.2026, 22.03.2026, 19.05.2026 and 03.06.2026 — "a series of publications in five parts, under the title *The Velotix Files*." Those are the publication dates of Parts 1 through 5 of this series. All five.

Clause 2 asserts that the series attributes to the clients, expressly and by implication, deception of employees, unequal treatment, denial of fair process, breach of law, infringement of employees' rights, unlawful conduct, concealment of information, misuse of information, and "acts bearing criminal characteristics," while publishing information said to be false, distorted, biased and without factual basis.

Clause 3 says the publications are false, distorted and tendentious, lacking factual foundation, presenting facts partially and misleadingly, omitting relevant data, and creating a false impression in the mind of the reasonable reader.

Clause 5 turns to my employment. It states that I worked at Velotix from 27 October 2024 as a Machine Learning Engineer; that I was exposed to the company's business, commercial, technological and internal information; and that my employment agreement obliged me to maintain confidentiality, to refrain from using the company's information for purposes other than my work, and — the letter's phrasing — to refrain from any act liable to harm the company's reputation and business interests. Some of these undertakings, it says, continue to apply after the end of employment.

Clause 6 puts the damage at "millions of shekels": harm to reputation, harm to investor confidence, cancelled and frozen business relationships, harm to capital raising, and lost transactions and opportunities. No breakdown is given, and no figure other than that one.

## The Demands

Clause 7 requires, within twenty-four hours of receipt:

**A.** The immediate removal, without delay, of all the publications that are the subject of the letter — "and in this regard, all parts of the blog *The Velotix Files*" — from any website, blog, digital platform, social network, or any other publication medium under my control.

**B.** Every action within my power to remove the publications from search engines, digital archives, and any other source in which they appear.

**C.** Abstention from any further publication, directly or indirectly, concerning the clients.

Clause 8 states that failing full compliance within twenty-four hours, a claim will be filed against me on Sunday 12 July 2026, without further warning, in the District Court — seeking declaratory relief, an injunction, a mandatory order, publication of a correction and an apology, and full compensation for damages amounting to millions of shekels, together with costs and fees.

Clause 9 states that the letter is a final warning, and that no further warning, extension, or additional opportunity will be given before proceedings are taken.

## What the Letter Does Not Say

Across two pages, ten clauses, and five pleaded causes of action, the letter does not quote a single sentence from the series.

It does not identify one statement of fact and assert that the statement is untrue. It does not say that the Ministry of Labour's enforcement findings, set out in Part 1, are wrong, or that they were misdescribed. It does not say that the insolvency petition, חדל"ת 60765-02-26, filed by thirteen former employees and managers, does not exist or does not say what Part 1 says it says. It does not dispute the Delaware franchise tax delinquency, the registry fees, the archived advertising copy, the ISA licence held by PipelBiz, the subscriber count of the Telegram channel, the contents of the *TheMarker* report of 9 March 2026, or the contents of the *Maariv* report of 3 June 2026 that forms the basis of Part 5.

It does not offer a correction. It does not identify anything to correct.

What it asks for is the deletion of all five parts, and their removal from search engines and from archives.

A demand that a publication be corrected identifies the error. A demand that a publication cease to exist, and that the record of it be scrubbed from the archives, does not need to.

## The Fourth Part

Clause 1 lists 19.05.2026 among the publications to be deleted.

The publication of 19 May 2026 is [Part 4](/posts/velotix-the-takedown/). Part 4 is not about Velotix's finances, or its employees, or its investors. Part 4 is the post that documents the fraudulent DMCA notice — filed on 18 May 2026 from a disposable email address at a Chittagong postal address, citing as the infringed "original work" a *TheMarker* column about the former Israeli finance minister Moshe Kahlon that mentions neither Velotix nor Dr Hod — which attempted to remove Part 1 from the internet.

The letter demands the removal of the post about the attempted removal.

It offers no view on the DMCA notice, does not mention it, and does not say who filed it.

## The Timing

The letter is dated Thursday 9 July. It arrived at 16:37 on Friday 10 July. It allows twenty-four hours. It threatens a claim on Sunday 12 July.

The Israeli working week runs Sunday to Thursday. From 16:37 on Friday until the close of Shabbat on Saturday night, the offices of Israeli advocates are shut. The twenty-four hours therefore expire on Saturday afternoon, during Shabbat. The first hour at which a recipient of this letter could ordinarily obtain legal advice is Sunday morning — which is the day, and by clause 9 the day without extension, on which the claim is to be filed.

The courier's message closes: *Shabbat shalom.*

## The Law

Under the Defamation (Prohibition) Law, 5725–1965, it is a defence that the publication was true and that there was public interest in its publication. The burden of proving truth rests on the defendant. This series was assembled from Ministry of Labour enforcement findings, court filings, the Companies Registrar, Delaware corporate records, the Israel Securities Authority's public register, archived advertising, and the published reporting of *TheMarker* and *Maariv*. Every document it relies on is cited in it.

Two things about the letter are worth stating plainly, because they pull in opposite directions.

The first is that a defamation claim requires the identification of the defamatory words. Should a claim be filed on Sunday, it will have to specify what this letter does not.

The second is that the claim under clause 5 — breach of a confidentiality undertaking — is not a defamation claim, and truth is not an answer to it. Whether that undertaking says what the letter says it says, and whether it reaches material of the kind published here, are questions for the contract and for a court. This post takes no position on them and neither should a reader.

Israel has no anti-SLAPP statute. There is no special motion, no early strike, no automatic award of costs to a defendant who has published the truth about a company and is sued for it. The remedy for a meritless claim in Israel is to defend it, at length and at one's own expense, and to ask for costs at the end. This is a fact about Israeli procedure and not a complaint about it.
