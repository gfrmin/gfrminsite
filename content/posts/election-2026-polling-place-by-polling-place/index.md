*Why the live projection failed on the night: [Station by Station](/posts/election-night-station-by-station/).*

{{< callout type="note" >}}
**In short.** From the committee's final count. Town turnout: [bechirot.fyi](https://bechirot.fyi/en/localities).

- **Netanyahu bloc: 53 seats**, down from 64 and eight short of a majority.
- **Arab turnout rose by 25 points**, to 78.5%. With the Arab parties running together, the Arab lists go from 10 seats to 19.
- **Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) turnout stood still, and Shas lost ground.** Turnout was flat while it rose elsewhere, and Shas fell in every Haredi town, from 11 seats to 6.
- **In the settlements, right-wing votes were lost.** They went to small lists below the 3.25% threshold and elected no one.
- **Druze towns moved towards Likud**, against the national tide.
- **In the mixed cities the Netanyahu bloc fell twice as fast or more** in the Jewish neighbourhoods as in the rest of the country.
{{< /callout >}}

A national result is an average, and an average hides things moving in opposite directions. On the final count, Israel's election on 27 October moved the Netanyahu bloc (Likud, Otzma Yehudit, Religious Zionism, Shas and United Torah Judaism) from 64 seats to 53, eight short of the 61 a government needs in the 120-seat Knesset. That is the headline. Underneath it, some places moved much further than the country did, some moved the other way, and in one group of towns turnout did not move at all while it rose everywhere else.

*Updated 30 October, on the final count.* These findings were first published on the 28th from the committee's results file of 10:02, Israel time, with 3.76 million valid votes counted and the seats a projection. Every figure below is now from the final count, in the committee's file of 00:08 on the 30th: 5.69 million valid votes, including the envelope ballots cast by soldiers, diplomats, prisoners and hospital patients. The official result goes to the President on 4 November. The Netanyahu bloc ends on 53 seats, not the projected 54, the Arab lists on 19 and Shas on 6. Arab turnout rose by 25 points, not 26, and three of the places named have dropped out of their lists: Hura from the largest rises in Arab turnout, Kiryat Malakhi from the top of Otzma Yehudit and Religious Zionism's gains, and Netanya from the smallest falls for the Netanyahu bloc in the big cities.

## Arab turnout jumped by twenty-five points

On the final count national turnout was 78.0%, against 70.6% in 2022. By far the largest rise came from one community. In polling places where the Arab lists won a majority in 2022, turnout went from 53.5% to 78.5%. The polling-place figures here cover only the places matched across the two elections, and none of the envelope ballots, so they compare with each other and not with the national figure.

{{< stat-chart id="turnout" >}}

The Netanyahu bloc lost least where turnout rose most:

| Polling places, by who won them in 2022 | Netanyahu bloc, change in share |
|---|---|
| The Arab lists | −1.2 points |
| Shas and UTJ | −3.6 points |
| Everywhere else | −4.5 points |

Turnout rose most in the Triangle, the band of Arab towns along the old Green Line in the centre of the country, and among the Bedouin of the Negev: 42 points in Umm al-Fahm, 39 in Fureidis, 37 in Ma'ale Iron and Jatt, 36 in Laqye. It rose least in Galilee towns with large Christian and Druze populations, 14 points in Mi'elya and Kafr Yasif, and in the Arab neighbourhoods of Haifa and Tel Aviv-Yafo, 11 and 12.

{{< stat-chart id="arab-rise" >}}

Turnout was half the story. The other half is that the Arab parties ran together. A list needs 3.25% of the national vote to win any seats. In 2022 Balad, one of the Arab parties, ran alone, took 2.9%, and elected no one. This time the Joint List, the alliance of the other Arab parties, won 55% of the vote in Arab towns, and Ra'am, the United Arab List, most of the rest. The Arab lists won 837,000 votes, against 511,000 in 2022, and 19 seats against 10.

## Haredi turnout stood still, and Shas lost ground

In Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) polling places turnout did not rise at all: 73.4% in 2022, 72.8% now. In Beit Shemesh's Haredi neighbourhoods it fell six points. Those who did vote turned away from Shas, whose share fell in every Haredi town with enough polling places to compare: by 10.5 points in Beit Shemesh, 8.0 in Betar Illit, 7.0 in Tiberias and 6.6 in El'ad. Nationally Shas won six seats, against eleven in 2022.

{{< stat-chart id="shas-fall" >}}

Some of that vote went to Itamar Ben Gvir's Otzma Yehudit, which took 10.1% in El'ad and 7.1% in Betar Illit. In 2022 it ran on a joint list with Religious Zionism, so there is no separate figure to compare. Taken together, the two parties gained most in a few religious communities and development towns, the small towns built for immigrants in the 1950s: 9.8 points in Yad Binyamin, 7.7 in Netivot, 5.2 in Kiryat Malakhi. In other development towns they barely moved: 2.6 in Sderot, about one in Ofakim and Yeroham, and a small fall in Dimona.

## The settlements lost votes below the threshold

The Netanyahu bloc fell furthest in some West Bank settlements, but not because their voters crossed over. Their votes went to small lists that will not enter the Knesset. Amcha Yisrael, Ofer Winter's list, took 11.9% in Kfar Adumim and 9.1% in Efrat. In Eli, Amcha Yisrael and Noam together took 14.7%. Amcha Yisrael won 96,000 votes nationally, 1.7%, short of the threshold. Those votes elected no one.

## Druze towns went the other way

While the country moved away from Likud, Druze towns moved towards it. Likud rose 12 points in Beit Jann, 7 in Daliyat al-Karmel and 6 in Hurfeish. Turnout rose too, by 22 points in Isifya, 19 in Mughar and 13 in Yirka, far more than in the Jewish towns around them. Kisra-Sumei shows even larger changes, but there it is 2022 that was unusual: turnout fell to 32% that year and National Unity came first. In 2026 the town voted much as it did in 2021.

## In the mixed cities, two stories at once

Lod, Akko and Ramla, mixed cities with Jewish and Arab neighbourhoods, look at first like the Arab turnout story. In their Arab neighbourhoods turnout rose 16 to 22 points and the Arab lists gained 7 to 9. But the Netanyahu bloc also fell in the cities' other neighbourhoods: by 12.3 points in Lod, 9.0 in Akko and 8.9 in Ramla, two to nearly three times its fall elsewhere in the country. A city's total mixes the two. Only the polling places separate them.

{{< stat-chart id="mixed" >}}

Elsewhere the change was more even. Likud fell most in Nof HaGalil (6.9 points), Ariel (6.7) and Modi'in (6.2), and held or gained in Kiryat Gat, Netanya and Ashdod. In the big cities the bloc as a whole fell by between 1.8 points (Tel Aviv) and 6.8 (Rishon LeZion).

## The new parties sit where the old ones did

[bechirot.fyi](https://bechirot.fyi/en/map/places) draws a political map from the votes alone: places that vote alike sit close together, and each party sits where its voters are. Its polling-place map runs to 2022. Fitted the same way to the final 2026 count, a map drawn from polling places rather than towns puts Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar! and Naftali Bennett's Together almost on top of each other, where National Unity sat in 2022, and not where Yesh Atid did. Eisenkot was third on National Unity's list in 2022.

## How the polls did

Each poll is scored two ways. The first is how many seats it put on the wrong list: half the total of its misses, list by list, because every seat given to one list is a seat taken from another. The second is how far off it was on the Netanyahu bloc.

The exit polls, at 22:00:

| Channel | Seats on the wrong list | Netanyahu bloc |
|---|---|---|
| Kan 11 | 5 | 3 too few |
| Channel 13 | 5 | 3 too few |
| Channel 12 | 6 | 3 too few |
| i24NEWS | 6 | 2 too many |
| Channel 14 | 8 | 7 too many |

Two channels revised their polls later that night. Channel 13 moved to within two seats on the bloc. Channel 14's revision gave the bloc 62 and a majority, nine seats too many.

Before polling day, the best of each pollster's last polls was i24NEWS's, by Direct Polls, on 20 October: seven seats on the wrong list, the bloc five too high. The worst was Channel 16's, by Maagar Mochot, on the same day: fourteen seats on the wrong list, the bloc seven too low. The rest, from Channel 12, Channel 13, Israel Hayom, Kan, Zman Yisrael, Maariv and Walla, put eleven to thirteen seats on the wrong list.

The rankings matter less than what the polls got wrong together. On average the last polls had Likud five or six seats too low, and Ra'am and the Joint List more than two seats too low each: the same rise in Arab turnout this post began with, which no poll saw coming. They also gave The Reservists more than two seats, a list now below the threshold. The exit polls missed by about half as much, and their largest remaining misses were small: a seat or more too many each for Shas, the Democrats and Together, a seat too few each for Ra'am and the Joint List.

## The call that was furthest out

Of every call made on the night, one was plainly wrong. Channel 14's 22:00 poll gave Yisrael Beiteinu four seats; its revision put the party below the threshold. On the count, Yisrael Beiteinu has 337,413 votes, 5.9% of the total. The threshold is 3.25%, or about 185,000 votes. The party cleared it almost twice over and has seven seats.

## If there were no threshold

The threshold decides who gets in at all, so it is fair to ask what it changed. Here are the same votes under three sets of rules:

| | Netanyahu bloc | Change bloc | Arab lists | Others |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The law: a 3.25% threshold, with surplus agreements | 53 | 48 | 19 | — |
| No threshold, surplus agreements kept | 51 | 46 | 18 | 5 |
| No threshold and no agreements | 51 | 46 | 18 | 5 |

Without a threshold, five seats would go to two lists that do not get in. The Reservists, with HaKalkalit, have 2.7% of the vote and would get three. Amcha Yisrael, Ofer Winter's list, has 1.7% and would get two. Those seats come out of all three groups: with the agreements kept, Likud and Yashar! would have two fewer each, and Ra'am one fewer.

So the threshold does not favour either side over the other. It gives each large bloc two seats and the Arab lists one. What it does is decide that 307,958 votes, 5.4% of all those cast, elect no one. Amcha Yisrael's two seats would have sat on the right, which is the settlement vote this post found going to waste. The Reservists' three are not counted in either bloc, because the newspapers' grouping does not place them.

## How these numbers were made

Each comparison is a polling place against itself: the same place, in the same neighbourhood, in 2022 and in 2026. The committee renumbers its ballot boxes between elections, splitting and merging them, but the whole number of a ballot box (the 20 in 20.3) identifies the polling place, and that carries over. Matched that way, 5,029 polling places in 1,084 towns line up, holding 2.74 million votes.

As a check, I compared how far each matched place leaned away from the rest of its town in 2022 with how far it leaned in 2026. The correlation is 0.96 for the Haredi parties, 0.96 for the Arab parties and 0.91 for Likud. Pairing each place with the next number in the same town instead gives 0.19, 0.35 and 0.26, so the matches are the same neighbourhoods and not chance. Places that do not match, mostly new or renumbered ones, are left out, and so are the envelope ballots. The 652 places in the turnout chart are the polling places the Arab lists won in 2022. [Station by Station](/posts/election-night-station-by-station/) counts something else, the 1,606 ballot boxes that had an Arab majority in 2022, so the two numbers do not compare.
